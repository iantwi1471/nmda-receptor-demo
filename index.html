What you're looking at

  • Glutamate is released when the presynaptic neuron fires. Two molecules dock on the receptor.
  • Magnesium (Mg²⁺) plugs the pore at resting voltage. Depolarizing the postsynaptic membrane repels it out.
  • Calcium (Ca²⁺) flows in only while both conditions hold. In real neurons, this calcium signal triggers long-term potentiation (LTP), which strengthens the synapse.

Things to try

  • Fire the presynaptic neuron alone. Glutamate binds, but nothing gets through.
  • Depolarize alone. The magnesium leaves, but the gate stays shut.
  • Fire, wait until the purple bar is nearly empty, then depolarize. Calcium enters only during the brief overlap.
A simplified teaching model. Timings are not to scale, and the co-agonist (glycine or D-serine) is assumed to be present.
